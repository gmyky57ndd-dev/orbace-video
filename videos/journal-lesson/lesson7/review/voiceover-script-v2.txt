An Extreme puzzle begins simply enough. A few cross-hatching moves reveal a confined group: one and seven.

Then comes the fork. Two positions remain for two. We choose one—not because we know it's right, but because we can follow where it leads.

One placement forces another. Box six resolves. Still, no conflict.

The branch holds.

Now two boxes constrain seven in parallel. That fixes another seven, and the puzzle begins to open.

Soon, every cell is filled.

But completion isn't proof.

Return to the assumption. Trace it through the finished grid. No duplicate. No contradiction.

Verified.

Sometimes the branch doesn't break.

It leads all the way home.
